# Part 1 — Business Identity and Digital Store Positioning

## 1.1 What KitLuy is

> **KitLuy Suite is a Cambodia-first Digital Store operating system.**

A Partner creates the Digital Store first, selects exactly one primary vertical, configures its catalog, pricing, staff, payment, customer, fulfilment, and operating rules, then chooses how to operate:

- online-only through approved digital channels;
- through one or more optional physical Store Locations;
- or through a hybrid of physical and digital channels.

The Digital Store is the business control plane. A physical Store Location is an offline-capable execution environment. Store Hub is the local operational authority after provisioning. External channels are governed distribution surfaces; they do not own KitLuy operational truth.

## 1.2 Core positioning statement

**For Cambodian independent businesses, chains, and future multi-vertical operators that need reliable daily operations and digital growth, KitLuy is a Digital Store operating system that combines business setup, physical-store operations, offline continuity, management, customer commerce, payments, reporting, and governed integrations in one platform. Unlike a generic POS, marketplace account, or disconnected web store, KitLuy keeps the Partner's Digital Store, data, workflows, and operating authority intact across physical and digital channels.**

## 1.3 Brand promise

**Create digitally. Operate physically. Sell everywhere.**

The promise has three testable layers:

1. **Create digitally** — establish the Partner Account and Digital Store without requiring physical hardware.
2. **Operate physically** — provision certified Store Hub and terminals when a Location is needed; continue approved local operations during internet failure.
3. **Sell everywhere** — publish through KitLuy Storefront and approved connectors while KitLuy remains the source of truth.

## 1.4 What KitLuy is not

KitLuy is not:

- a generic cloud POS with Laundry labels added;
- a consumer marketplace that owns the merchant relationship;
- a WordPress/WooCommerce runtime;
- a statutory ERP or complete general ledger at launch;
- a merchant of record or payment facilitator under the current model;
- a hardware reseller without a software and support relationship;
- a single monolithic application;
- a system where HET Admin, Chain, Partner, Store staff, customer, and connector permissions are mixed;
- an AI system allowed to rewrite financial, payment, inventory, permission, compliance, or custody truth without human authorization;
- a platform that charges Partners to retrieve their own reporting, history, analytics, exports, or data.

## 1.5 Strategic thesis

KitLuy is built on six business bets:

1. **Operational value is the best initial wedge.** Businesses retain a platform that makes the daily workflow faster, safer, and more measurable.
2. **Laundry creates a defensible first vertical.** Per-weight/per-piece pricing, garments, tags, damage evidence, production, Ready custody, pickup control, and offline operation require depth generic POS products often lack.
3. **The Digital Store should outlive every Location and channel.** A Partner can start online-only, add Locations, change channels, or expand without recreating the business identity and operating model.
4. **Offline reliability is a commercial differentiator.** Store Hub protects the customer experience and revenue when internet connectivity is degraded.
5. **Shared Core creates vertical leverage.** Each completed phase should reduce the cost and risk of the next phase without contaminating Core with vertical-specific terminology.
6. **Governed operational data compounds.** Structured transactions, customers, inventory, capacity, files, events, devices, and support history enable better reporting and permission-scoped AI.

## 1.6 Cambodia-first commitments

KitLuy must be commercially and operationally designed for:

- Khmer and English;
- KHR and USD;
- `Asia/Phnom_Penh`;
- KHQR and approved Cambodian payment providers;
- local phone-number and identity practices;
- intermittent connectivity and power conditions;
- local hardware availability, import cost, warranty, repair, and replacement realities;
- local onboarding, training, field support, and business buying behavior;
- legal, privacy, tax, payment, employment, and consumer obligations approved by qualified humans.

# Part 2 — Eight-Phase Vertical Roadmap

## 2.1 Locked sequence

The roadmap order is locked. A change requires a versioned owner decision.

| Phase | Vertical | Core customer promise | Commercial release outcome |
|---:|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Laundry Stores and Shops** | Run intake, payment, production, custody, Ready storage, pickup, customer communication, reporting, and offline operations correctly. | A deployable, supportable, profitable-or-evidence-backed Laundry offer with T1–T4 and repeatable activation. |
| **2** | **Café and Restaurant Stores** | Run dine-in, takeaway, delivery, kitchen production, checks/tabs, shifts, and online ordering. | A hospitality offer proven on top of shared Core without weakening Laundry. |
| **3** | **Online Retailers and eCommerce Businesses** | Create storefronts, sell online, manage checkout, fulfilment, customers, subscriptions, domains, and connectors. | A Digital Store commerce platform with governed APIs and sustainable channel economics. |
| **4** | **Convenience Stores** | Perform fast barcode checkout, purchasing, inventory, expiry, shifts, cash, labels, loyalty, and multi-tender. | A high-throughput small-retail offer with dependable stock and cash control. |
| **5** | **Drugstores and Pharmacies** | Operate medicine, batch/lot, expiry, FEFO, controlled items, recalls, privacy, approvals, and restricted returns. | A compliance-aware operations product that never replaces pharmacist judgment. |
| **6** | **Department Stores** | Coordinate departments, catalogs, price books, promotions, transfers, returns, gift value, loyalty, commissions, and reporting. | A multi-department commercial offer with chain-grade governance and finance controls. |
| **7** | **Grocery Stores** | Manage produce, weighted goods, scales, expiry, FEFO, waste, fresh production, counts, and margin. | A fresh-goods offer with trusted wastage, margin, and inventory evidence. |
| **8** | **Supermarkets** | Run high-volume registers, central catalog, warehouse/store replenishment, promotions, self-checkout, cash office, and regional reporting. | An enterprise-grade offer with high availability, distribution, approvals, and regional operations. |

## 2.2 Vertical rule

- Every Digital Store belongs to exactly one primary vertical.
- A Partner operating different business types creates separate Digital Stores under the same Tenant or Partner Account.
- A later vertical may reuse Core but may not change the meaning of an existing vertical's records, workflows, reports, or hardware profiles.
- Vertical terminology belongs in the vertical layer, not hardcoded into shared Core.

## 2.3 Vertical investment rule

A later vertical may enter active build only when:

- the current phase meets its technical and commercial exit criteria;
- the shared foundation required for the next phase is identified;
- the next phase has named product, engineering, commercial, operations, support, finance, and legal owners;
- the expansion does not consume critical capacity needed to stabilize earlier live verticals;
- an approved budget and evidence plan exist.

## 2.4 Scope-control rule

Build shared capability only to the level required by the active vertical and confirmed near-term reuse. Record future requirements without speculative implementation. Do not build an enterprise supermarket platform during the Laundry phase merely because it may be useful later.

## 4.3 Primary jobs to be done

### Partner owner

- Know what is happening without standing at the counter.
- Trust sales, payment, pickup, issues, capacity, and staff records.
- Keep operating during internet problems.
- Expand to additional Locations or channels without rebuilding the business.

### Store manager

- Configure services, prices, staff, payments, capacity, and operating rules.
- Detect overdue, blocked, or risky Bookings.

- Perform each task quickly with role-specific screens and clear custody.

- Standardize what should be standardized while allowing audited local operating exceptions.
- Compare Stores using trusted, fresh, consistently defined reporting.

### Customer

- Prepare information before counter service.
- See clear services, totals, payment state, queue, and pickup information.
- Trust that garments and payments are correctly handled.

### HET operator

- Onboard, provision, support, monitor, update, and recover Stores safely with least privilege and evidence.

# Part 6 — Hardware Commercial Model

## 6.1 Commercial purpose

Hardware is not a standalone commodity line. It is the certified physical execution layer of a KitLuy Digital Store and Store Location. The hardware model must protect reliability, security, supportability, replacement, and lifecycle control.

## 6.2 Phase 1 reference profiles

| Profile | Reference hardware | Operational role |
|---|---|---|
| Store Hub | Raspberry Pi 5, 8 GB RAM, industrial/high-endurance 256 GB or greater NVMe, active cooling; UPS recommended | Local PostgreSQL, Edge API, sync, files, device control, printing coordination, and release distribution. |
| Front Counter Terminal | Raspberry Pi 5, 4 GB or approved terminal, operator display/touchscreen, paired customer-facing display | T1 POS Cashier / Intake plus paired T2 Customer Display Screen. |
| Ready/Pickup Terminal | Raspberry Pi 5, 4 GB or approved terminal, scanner and optional secondary display | Separate T3 Clean & Ready Scan-In and T4 Customer Pickup Scan-Out modes. |
| Peripherals | Certified receipt/tag printers, barcode/QR scanners, scale where required, networking, UPS/accessories | Approved by Store workflow and hardware support matrix. |

Exact certified models, suppliers, quantities, landed costs, and substitution rules remain `[REQUIRED: approved BOM and certification matrix]`.

## 6.3 Closed managed-device rule

For certified Phase 1 physical operation:

- the Store Hub must be HET-enrolled and pre-installed;
- device identity uses approved hardware attributes, certificates, and keys;
- cloned, unregistered, tampered, or mismatched devices fail closed or enter quarantine;
- Store staff do not replace or reimage Hub NVMe media independently;
- damaged storage or board identity is handled through HET repair/re-registration or replacement-first recovery;
- unsupported hardware may be used only in development/testing unless separately certified;
- a physical Store cannot pass certified go-live with unsupported core hardware.

## 6.4 Allowed commercial forms

The price book may offer one or more of the following after finance/legal approval:

- customers' personal data is used only for authorized business and platform purposes;
- HET does not sell Partner or customer data to advertisers;
- connector access requires consent, scope, purpose, retention, and revocation;
- Chain access is limited to authorized participating Digital Stores/Locations and approved governance relationships;

# Part 15 — Phase-Level Commercial Exit Criteria

## 15.1 Common exit rule

A vertical phase is not commercially complete because the software demo works. It exits only when the owner approves evidence across:

1. **Authority** — scope, exclusions, legal/commercial decisions, and product boundaries are versioned.
2. **Customer value** — target customers demonstrate a material job solved.
3. **Product** — critical workflows and required interfaces are implemented and verified.
4. **Offline/edge** — required local operation, sync, recovery, and hardware pass.
5. **Activation** — onboarding and go-live are repeatable with documented effort.
6. **Support** — severity, escalation, runbooks, staffing, and diagnostics are ready.
7. **Commercial** — package, price, contract, billing, and discount rules are approved.
8. **Economics** — direct cost, margin, support load, hardware, and cash effects are measured.
9. **Retention** — pilot/live cohort shows evidence of ongoing use and renewal potential.
10. **Security/legal** — privacy, payment, compliance, data, and risk controls pass.
11. **Operations** — monitoring, backup, restore, spares, replacement, and incident response are ready.
12. **Documentation** — Rebuild and Business Bibles, contracts, SOPs, QA, and go-live evidence are current.
13. **Rebuild Test** — one qualified engineer/operator can reconstruct and operate the vertical.
14. **Capacity** — the next phase can begin without destabilizing earlier live customers.

All numeric thresholds require an approved phase scorecard.

## 15.2 Phase 1 — Laundry exit criteria

Laundry may exit Phase 1 when:

- T1 POS Cashier/Intake, T2 Customer Display, T3 Ready Scan-In, and T4 Pickup Scan-Out pass integrated operation;

- Storefront QR/Telegram pre-intake and queue either passes its approved launch/pilot scope or remains clearly feature-gated;
- Store Hub offline operation, synchronization, signed releases, security, replacement, and recovery pass;
- physical and digital-only activation paths are documented;
- at least `[REQUIRED]` representative pilot Stores and `[REQUIRED]` Chain/multi-Store evidence, if Chain GA is claimed, are approved;
- activation time, first value, support load, hardware failure, and Store contribution are measured;

## 15.10 Phase decision outcomes

- **Exit / proceed** — criteria pass; next phase may enter active investment.
- **Limited GA** — sell only to approved segment/region/package while specific evidence closes.
- **Continue pilot** — value exists but repeatability/economics/support are incomplete.
- **Pause** — capacity, reliability, legal, security, or economics do not support expansion.
- **Narrow scope** — remove nonessential breadth and complete the commercial core.
- **Stop** — evidence does not support continued investment.
