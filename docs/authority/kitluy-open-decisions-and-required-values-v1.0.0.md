| KLREQ-031 | **No owner policy governs device private-key retention or destruction.** A renewal supersedes the previous generation's key, and nothing rules on how long that key is kept, what suspends its destruction, or who may authorize it. Destruction is IRREVERSIBLE and it is not revocation: destroying a key does not revoke the credential it backed, and retiring an overlapped credential does not destroy a key. Three distinct controls, and the missing one must not be substituted by either of the others | **RESOLVED / OWNER-APPROVED 2026-07-29** — `KLD-2026-07-29-DEVICE-KEY-DESTRUCTION-001`, approved as written without amendment. Approved values: superseded retention **30 days after `overlap_ends_at`**, abandoned retention **7 days after `abandoned_at`**, recovery retention **14 days after the latest verified terminal recovery**, eligibility = the LATEST applicable of those, approval validity **24 hours**, maximum execution attempts **5**, four-eyes REQUIRED with requester ≠ approver, re-authentication and reason REQUIRED, `automatic_provider_destruction = false`. Incident, legal and audit holds suspend eligibility, execution, approval use and retry; releasing a hold invalidates any prior approval and requires fresh eligibility and approval. Provider evidence (receipt or attestation, or a deterministic response with request id, key reference, timestamp and audit correlation) is REQUIRED before the database may mark a key destroyed. Permission keys `fleet.device_key.destruction_request`, `fleet.device_key.destruction_approve` and `fleet.device_key.hold_manage` must be registered through the governed RBAC registry before pilot or production use; until registered, destruction fails closed. **Approval closes the missing-policy requirement ONLY (§17) and promotes no implementation.** |
| KLREQ-032 | **Device credential revocation: who may approve it, and what it reaches.** Group 0136 shipped the governed revocation path requiring four-eyes for EVERY reason, because no owner decision narrowed the list; and which reasons condemn the whole device rather than the credential was a repository default only. | **RESOLVED / OWNER-APPROVED 2026-07-29** — `KLD-2026-07-29-DEVICE-CREDENTIAL-REVOCATION-001`, approved as written. Normal revocations need two distinct authorized humans BEFORE execution. An emergency immediate path exists ONLY for `KEY_COMPROMISE`, `DEVICE_LOST`, `DEVICE_STOLEN`, `PROVIDER_COMPROMISE` and `SECURITY_INCIDENT`, requiring a CISO or incident commander, re-authentication, a reason, an incident reference, immutable audit, and second-person post-approval within **4 hours**. **A late, missing or refused post-approval never reverses the revocation** — the case enters `MANUAL_SECURITY_REVIEW` and escalates, because an auto-reversing revocation would let an attacker who merely delays the approver get the credential back. `ASSIGNMENT_INVALIDATED`, `CERTIFICATE_MISISSUANCE`, `ADMINISTRATIVE_REPLACEMENT` and `OTHER_APPROVED_REASON` always require approval first. **No machine-initiated revocation is approved for Phase 1.** Scope is derived from the REASON, not chosen by the caller (see the decision §3 table); `PROVIDER_COMPROMISE` and `SECURITY_INCIDENT` may never use an unrestricted wildcard and must record an explicit affected set; unknown scope FAILS CLOSED to manual review. | KLREQ-034 | **Which device lifecycle states must deny certificate validation at the edge.** WS-11-T003 Step 4 Phase D built the first database-backed `RevocationLookup` (`pg-revocation-lookup.ts`, RC-027) and had to decide what "this DEVICE is revoked" means, separately from "this CREDENTIAL is revoked".
